Etching gallium arsenide with light

    Before you start

    What this lab is about, and a prediction to test.

    Semiconductors respond to light. In this lab the response is chemical. Light lets dilute acid eat into GaAs, but only under the right conditions. Doping decides whether those conditions are met, so a purely optical experiment ends up telling you about electrical doping.

    Objective

    Photons meet a semiconductor

    Explore how light interacts with GaAs during photoelectrochemical (PEC) etching.

    Objective

    Doping changes the outcome

    Compare etch behavior in n-type and p-type GaAs under the same light and acid.

    Make a prediction

    Think through these before you touch a sample. Write your prediction down. You will check it against your data.

    • How much energy does a 635 nm photon carry, and how does it compare with the GaAs bandgap?
    • Dissolving a semiconductor means oxidizing it. Which carrier, electron or hole, can do that?
    • Sketch the bands of n-type and p-type GaAs touching a liquid. Which way does each bend at the surface?
    • What should a scan of an area the laser never touched look like?

    Safety

    Acid, an arsenic compound and lasers share one bench. Read this and tick every box. The etch timer stays locked until you do.

    Acid

    1:10 HCl in water

    Dilute, but it still burns eyes and irritates skin. Your TF supplies it. Acid always goes into water, never the reverse.

    Material

    Gallium arsenide

    Intact pieces are low risk with gloves on. Dust and dissolved material are the hazard. Scribe gently and wipe up with a damp wipe.

    Light

    635 nm and 980 nm lasers

    Reflections come off the liquid, the wafer and the glass. The 980 nm beam is invisible, and your blink reflex will not react to it.

    Question 5 is a thought experiment

    The report asks what stronger acid would do. Answer it on paper only. Never mix or use a 1:2 or 1:1 solution.

    Write the group report

    One submission per group with every member’s name and an answer to each question, backed by your own data.

    20pts

    Does n-type etch differently from p-type?

    Do you see a difference in etching behavior between n-type and p-type GaAs, and what is it?

    Compare exposed and unexposed scans of each type with the same baseline and color scale. Quote numbers from profiles or statistics.

    15pts

    Etch depth and rate

    Where you see etching, plot depth against time and extrapolate an etch rate. Choose and state your baseline. Is it linear? Should it be? What errors are in your measurements or extrapolation?

    Explain what a nonzero intercept would mean. Separate measurement error from variation in the experiment itself.

    10pts

    The mechanism

    Given what you observed, explain the mechanism behind PEC etching of GaAs with a 635 nm laser.

    Follow one photon from the laser to a dissolved product. Draw a band diagram for each doping type and say where each carrier goes.

    10pts

    A different wavelength

    Would a 300 nm or a 1000 nm laser work? Why or why not? Stay consistent with your answer to Question 3.

    Use the photon energy check in chapter 1. Consider whether the light is absorbed and also where.

    10pts

    Stronger acid

    What would happen with a 1:2 or 1:1 HCl to water dilution? Give reasons for your hypothesis.

    Name at least two things that change as acid gets stronger and say which matters most. Hypothesis only. Do not test it.

    If something looks wrong

    Common snags and what to do. When in doubt, ask your TF.

    A laser turned off during the etch

    The touch sensor needs steady contact with the foil. Rewrap it, note the gap in your log and tell your TF.

    A sample moved in the dish

    Turn the lasers off first by removing the foil. Tell your TF, note the time, and decide together how to continue.

    There are bubbles on a sample

    Log the time and how heavy they are, then tell your TF.

    We can’t find the etch on the profilometer

    Go back to your corner notes and scan near where the beam hit. Lab staff can help you find the edge of the pit.

    No interference bands appear

    Turn the jog wheel slowly through focus. Check that the sample is clean, dry and flat. Then ask lab staff.

    The n-type and p-type scans look the same

    Confirm you scanned the right corner and did not swap samples. Compare with the unexposed scans. Then report what you saw honestly. An unexpected result still counts.

    We don’t have data for the other etch times

    Ask your TF for the class data sheet. Each group runs only one etch time.
