Permitted project work

The bounds every project in the EE Active Learning Labs must stay within.

Working here means accepting real risk. Following these guidelines does not remove every hazard of electrical and mechanical work. By taking on a project you acknowledge that you are working with energized circuits, moving mechanical parts and thermal hazards, and you take full responsibility for your personal safety and the integrity of your design. The EE Active Learning Labs, their staff and the University disclaim liability for injury, property damage or equipment failure caused by improper tool use, unauthorized circuit changes, or not following the voltage, current and battery protocols below. Going outside these bounds is at your own risk and can lead to immediate loss of lab privileges.

Staff may ask for a safety testing SOP, a defined protocol for how you will work on your project safely.

Section 1

Electrical power

All projects must stay within the safety and power limits of lab resources. No external high-voltage sources or unapproved high-current transformers.

  • Voltage ceilingNo circuit node may exceed 30 V DC or 25 V AC peak. This includes the supply input, series-chained channels and any internally boosted voltages, such as from transformers or DC-DC converters.
  • Current limitIndividual circuits must not draw more than 3 A.
  • Approved sources onlyUse only lab-approved DC bench supplies or isolated battery packs. AC mains-derived high-voltage sources, unapproved high-current transformers and external wall adapters are not allowed.
  • Protections onConfigure and enable over-voltage (OVP) and over-current (OCP) protection on the power supply before energizing any circuit.
  • No voltage boosting without approvalDo not use transformers, boost converters, capacitive charge pumps or inductive switching to raise voltages inside a circuit without staff approval.
Section 2

Human contact and bio-electrical work

Direct electrical interaction with the human body is prohibited, to prevent shock or cardiac interference.

  • No current injectionNo project may intentionally source, sink or leak current through a person.
  • No body in the circuitThe human body may not be part of a resistive or capacitive circuit path in any project powered by mains-connected equipment.
  • Isolate bio-sensingProjects that sense bio-potentials, such as heart rate monitors, must be battery-powered only and electrically isolated from the power supply and oscilloscope.
Section 3

General limits

  • HeatAny component above 60 °C must be shielded or actively cooled. Projects with open heating elements are suspended.
  • DronesNo flying drones or untethered flying vehicles inside the lab. Bench testing is allowed only with the vehicle clamped to the work surface and propeller guards installed.
  • Weapons and projectilesDevices meant to launch projectiles, such as coil guns or rail guns, and anything classed as a weapon are strictly forbidden.
  • Sound and lightNo sound levels or high frequencies that risk hearing damage. Shield high-intensity LEDs and strobes. Lasers above Class 1 need a specialized safety enclosure and staff approval.
  • Personal dataCollecting biometric or identifiable personal data requires IRB approval. Hidden surveillance projects (cameras or microphones) are not permitted.
  • Inductive loadsMotors, solenoids and other inductive loads must use flyback diodes to protect bench supply outputs from voltage spikes.
Section 4

Batteries

  • Never snip terminalsDo not cut battery leads with side cutters or scissors. Cutting both leads at once shorts them through the tool and can cause arcing or explosion.
  • No soldering to cellsNever solder directly to battery terminals or tabs. Use pre-crimped, approved connectors such as XT60 or JST.
  • Keep packs intactDo not remove protective shrink-wrap or re-pack cells. Mount batteries securely so loose tools or hardware cannot puncture them.
  • Charge only during supervised hoursNo overnight charging. Power down and disconnect all chargers before the lab closes.
  • Stay at the benchRemain at the bench for the whole charging cycle. No set-and-forget charging.
  • Approved chargers onlyBench power supplies must never be used as battery chargers. Use only dedicated balance chargers with thermal shut-off.
  • LiPo bagsCharge every lithium-polymer (LiPo) battery inside a flame-retardant charging bag.
  • Storage voltageIf a battery will sit unused for more than 48 hours, bring it to storage voltage (3.8 to 3.85 V per cell).
  • Check before useLook for swelling, sweet odors (electrolyte leak) or excess heat.
  • Dispose safelyMove damaged or unstable cells to the designated sand bucket and notify lab staff for hazardous waste processing.

  • IEC 62368-1, Audio/video, information and communication technology equipment, Part 1: Safety requirements (touch-temperature limits).
  • IEC 60825-1, Safety of laser products, Part 1: Equipment classification and requirements.
  • ANSI Z136.1, American National Standard for Safe Use of Lasers. Laser Institute of America.
  • National Fire Protection Association, Lithium-Ion Battery Safety Tip Sheet.
  • Horizon Hobby and HobbyKing, LiPo battery storage voltage guidance.
